# Max Strength Training

*Complete and self-contained set of rules for max strength training with free weights (barbell/dumbbell). All required information, including the repetition table, is contained in this document.*

## 0. F-Max and the Strength Test

Every percentage in this document refers to **F-Max**: the maximum load that applies to the exercise being performed.

- F-Max is not measured per exercise. It is derived from the barbell 1RM of the exercise's **movement direction** (knee, hip, horizontal push, …) and then converted for the **equipment variant** of the exercise. "The F-Max of the respective exercise" is therefore the equipment-adjusted max value for that exercise's direction.
- The strength test, the 1RM estimate, the equipment conversion factors, and the rounding rules are specified in [Weight Calculation from Strength Tests](./weight-calculation-from-strength-tests.md).
- F-Max is treated as constant while an exercise progresses. A new strength test replaces it; because progression is stored as percentages (Section 8), the ladder position is kept and only the resulting kg values change. The re-test cadence is not prescribed by this document.

## 1. Basic Rules

- All weights are percentages of the personal maximum (F-Max) of the respective exercise. Example: 80% at an F-Max of 100 kg = 80 kg.
- There are two levels: Advanced and Pro. They differ in number of sets, weights, and repetitions.
- Max strength training is only intended for ages 16 and up and only from the performance level Advanced or Pro.
- Each session consists of 2 warm-up sets + the defined working sets.
- Each working set follows a fixed ladder (weight × repetitions) that runs from light to heavy. The heavier the weight, the fewer the repetitions – the built-in safety margin (buffer) protects against injuries.
- Rating happens once after the last set of the exercise (too heavy / completed / too easy) – not after every set. The rating only takes effect in the next session.
- Each set has a fixed starting weight and a fixed maximum weight (Section 3). The sets progress in parallel along their own ladders, each only up to its maximum weight. The spread between the sets arises automatically from the different maximum weights; there is no additional spacing rule.
- If the last set reaches the end of its ladder and is rated "completed" or "too easy" there, the exercise is switched (top of the exercise, Section 6.1).
- Mirrored at the bottom: if the sets are already at the 65% floor and the exercise is rated "too heavy" again, the exercise is switched to an easier one (Section 6.2).
## 2. Warm-up (both levels)

Always 2 warm-up sets, calculated from the actually displayed (rounded) weight of the 1st working set:

| Warm-up set | Weight | Repetitions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50% of the 1st working set | 6 |
| 2 | 75% of the 1st working set | 5 |

The warm-up weights are then rounded per Section 8.

- Between the warm-up sets: 60 s rest. Also 60 s between the 2nd warm-up set and the 1st working set.
- Warm-up sets run without a support exercise (Section 7 applies only to working sets).
## 3. Set Structure

Each working set has a fixed starting weight and a fixed maximum weight. The starting values come exclusively from the following tables. The starting weight is the entry point of a new exercise, the maximum weight the ceiling of its ladder. Neither is a hard bound downward: a "too heavy" rating can push a set below its starting weight, down to the 65% floor (Section 5.1).

**Pro – 4 sets**

| Set | Starting weight | Maximum weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 75% | 75% |
| 2 | 77.5% | 80% |
| 3 | 80% | 85% |
| 4 | 80% | 90% (= final value of the exercise) |

**Advanced – 3 sets**

| Set | Starting weight | Maximum weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 70% | 70% |
| 2 | 72.5% | 75% |
| 3 | 75% | 80% (= final value of the exercise) |

Development of the set structure over the sessions: Pro from 75 / 77.5 / 80 / 80% to 75 / 80 / 85 / 90%. Advanced from 70 / 72.5 / 75% to 70 / 75 / 80%.

## 4. The Ladders

The repetition ladder shows the repetition count per buffer percentage. Throughout an exercise, athletes move "up" by proceeding through the columns until they reach the next row, where they start again in the highest buffer column of their level that has a value for that weight. The buffer is the percentage subtracted from the maximum an athlete can perform, as measured by the strength test.

### Repetition table (reps per weight and buffer column)

A higher buffer means more safety margin and therefore fewer repetitions. Pro uses the columns 15 / 10 / 5%, Advanced uses the columns 20 / 15 / 10%. A dash (–) means: not used for this level/weight.

| Weight | 20% (Advanced Only) | 15% | 10% | 5% (Pro Only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 65% | 3 | 5 | 6 | – |
| 67.5% | 3 | 5 | 6 | – |
| 70% | 3 | 5 | 6 | – |
| 72.5% | 2 | 3 | 5 | – |
| 75% | 2 | 3 | 5 | 6 |
| 77.5% | 2 | 3 | 5 | 6 |
| 80% | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| 82.5% | – | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| 85% | – | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| 87.5% | – | – | 1 | 2 |
| 90% | – | – | 1 | 2 |

The weights 65 – 67.5% serve only as a fallback for "too heavy" (Section 5.1).

### Derivation of the steps

At a fixed weight, moving one step further means more repetitions (switching from the higher to the lower buffer column). Once the repetitions at the current weight are maxed out, the weight increases by +2.5 percentage points of F-Max, and the repetitions start again in the highest buffer column of the level that has a value for the new weight.

There are two categories targeted here, "Advanced" and "Professionals (Pro)". Beginners do not perform this kind of exercise.

- Pro: per weight 15% → 10% → 5%. Exception: At 87.5% and 90% the 15% column is empty, so those weights start in the 10% column (87.5% × 1, 90% × 1).
- Advanced: per weight 20% → 15% → 10%. Exception: The very first step (set 1, 70%) starts in the 15% column (70% × 5); the 20% column is only used as the starting column from the next weight (72.5%) onward.
- A set never rises above its maximum weight (Section 3).
### Ladder: Pro

- **Set 1 (75%):** 75×3 → 75×5 → 75×6
- **Set 2 (77.5 → 80%):** 77.5×3 → 77.5×5 → 77.5×6 → 80×2 → 80×3 → 80×5
- **Set 3 (80 → 85%):** 80×2 → 80×3 → 80×5 → 82.5×1 → 82.5×2 → 82.5×3 → 85×1 → 85×2 → 85×3
- **Set 4 (80 → 90%):** 80×2 → 80×3 → 80×5 → 82.5×1 → 82.5×2 → 82.5×3 → 85×1 → 85×2 → 85×3 → 87.5×1 → 87.5×2 → 90×1 → 90×2 (final value → switch exercise)
### Ladder: Advanced

- **Set 1 (70%):** 70×5 → 70×6
- **Set 2 (72.5 → 75%):** 72.5×2 → 72.5×3 → 72.5×5 → 75×2 → 75×3 → 75×5
- **Set 3 (75 → 80%):** 75×2 → 75×3 → 75×5 → 77.5×2 → 77.5×3 → 77.5×5 → 80×1 → 80×2 → 80×3 (final value → switch exercise)
## 5. Feedback & Progression

After the last set of the exercise, feedback is given once (not per set): too easy, completed, or too heavy. It only takes effect in the next session; the current session remains unchanged.

**Definition of "step":** A step is one move on the ladder of the respective set (Section 4), read from left (light) to right (heavy) – i.e. one arrow (→) further in the step sequence. This always refers to the ladder, not to a row of the repetition table.

| Feedback | Effect on the next session |
|---|---|
| completed | 1 step further (one move → to the right on the ladder). |
| too easy | 2 steps further (two moves → to the right on the ladder). |
| too heavy | Weight −2.5 percentage points of F-Max, never below 65%. Already at 65% → switch to an easier exercise. See Sections 5.1 and 6.2. |

Both progression ratings clamp at the end of the ladder: a set advances as far as its own ladder allows and then stays at its final value. If only one step remains, "too easy" advances that one step; the surplus step is discarded, not carried over into the next session.

**Progression ("completed" / "too easy")**

- The progression happens in parallel: All sets that can still progress are progressed. Sets that have reached their maximum weight with the maximum number of repetitions remain at their final value.
- Since the maximum weights increase from set to set, lower sets reach their target earlier; over time, the progression concentrates on the higher sets.
- First session of a new exercise: each set starts at the first (leftmost) step of its ladder.
### 5.1 Behavior on "too heavy"

"too heavy" is not a step movement on the ladder, but a pure weight fallback:

- All working sets of the exercise are reduced by 2.5 percentage points of F-Max, but never below 65%. All sets are reduced together, so the weights never decrease from set to set. At the 65% floor, several sets can come to rest on the same weight.
- For each set, the person remains in the same buffer column of the repetition table (Section 4) as before the reduction. For the new, lower weight, the repetition count stored in that column is used. The ladder is not reset to the first step.
- If that column is empty for the new weight (the 5% column below 75%), the set moves one column up to the next higher buffer that has a value there and uses its repetition count. The set continues in that column from then on.
- Examples: 77.5% × 5 → 75% × 5 (10% column). 80% × 3 → 77.5% × 5 (10% column). Pro 75% × 6 (5% column) → 72.5% × 5 (10% column, since the 5% column is empty at 72.5%).
- After a successfully completed session, the normal parallel progression resumes.
- The 65% floor is a hard clamp on the weight only. Once a set sits at 65%, a further "too heavy" no longer changes weight, buffer column, or repetitions; it ends the exercise at the bottom instead (Section 6.2).

## 6. End of the Exercise

An exercise is left at either of its two ends, and both ends follow the same logic in opposite directions: move one level along the branch of the skill tree (see [Branches, Exercises, and Levels](../foundations/branches-exercises-and-levels.md)), harder at the top and easier at the bottom. In both cases the athlete's progression on the new exercise starts again at step 1 of every set's ladder, calculated from the F-Max of the new exercise. If no replacement exercise exists, nothing changes and the athlete stays at the current weight% / repetition level.

### 6.1 Top: last set maxed out and rated "completed" or "too easy"

Trigger: the last set stands at its last ladder step and is rated "completed" or "too easy" there:

- Pro: 90% × 2
- Advanced: 80% × 3

Effect, in this order:

1. Level up within the branch: the next higher level holding a different exercise (the harder variant of the same movement pattern).
2. If the branch has no higher level, an equivalent branch from the same skill tree is substituted (same muscle group / movement pattern).
3. If neither is available, the exercise is kept and remains at its final value; further "completed" or "too easy" ratings have no effect.

### 6.2 Bottom: floor reached and rated "too heavy"

Trigger: the working sets are already at the 65% floor (a previous "too heavy" reduction was clamped there, Section 5.1) and the exercise is rated "too heavy" again. The weight cannot go any lower, so the exercise itself is the wrong difficulty.

Effect, in this order:

1. Level down within the branch: the next lower level holding a different exercise (the easier variant of the same movement pattern).
2. If the branch is exhausted at the bottom (already level 1), an equivalent branch from the same skill tree is substituted at the same difficulty, i.e. a different movement of comparable demand instead of an easier variant.
3. If neither is available, the exercise is kept unchanged: it stays at 65% with the current repetitions, and further "too heavy" ratings have no effect. This is the mirror of the stalled final value in Section 6.1.

The rating at the bottom applies to the exercise as a whole, exactly as at the top: a single "too heavy" at the floor is enough, no repeated confirmation over several sessions is required.

## 7. Support Exercise Between the Sets

A supporting block is inserted between each working set. The final rest differs by level.

**Advanced (unchanged, ≈ 3 min)**

| Step | Sequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | 30 s rest |
| 2 | Support exercise – set 1 |
| 3 | 60 s rest |
| 4 | Support exercise – set 2 |
| 5 | 30 s rest |

**Pro (adjusted – longer final rest due to higher loads)**

| Step | Sequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | 30 s rest |
| 2 | Support exercise – set 1 |
| 3 | 60 s rest |
| 4 | Support exercise – set 2 |
| 5 | 90 s rest (instead of 30 s) |

For the support exercise: 2 sets of the same exercise; bilateral exercise 8 repetitions or 30 s duration; left/right exercise 1 set per side per round. The specific exercise is freely selectable. Warm-up sets are excluded (Section 2).

**Pause between two working sets** is the whole block above, support sets included, counted from the end of one working set to the start of the next:

| Level | Pause between working sets |
|---|---|
| Advanced | 3 min (180 s) |
| Pro | 4 min (240 s) – the 60 s longer final rest |

After the last working set of an exercise no supporting block follows. The pause before the next exercise starts is **2 min (120 s)**, at both levels.

## 8. Rounding to Loadable Weights

- The progression is tracked internally as exact percentages of F-Max. Each set value is calculated independently from F-Max and the intended percentage.
- Rounding follows the general rules in [Weight Calculation from Strength Tests](./weight-calculation-from-strength-tests.md); max strength has no rounding rule of its own.
- Further progression does not continue calculating with the rounded kg value, but based on the stored percentage step.
- Warm-up weights are calculated from the displayed, rounded weight of the 1st working set and rounded in the same way.
## 9. Complete Examples (F-Max = 100 kg)

Since F-Max = 100 kg, here: % = kg.

### 9.1 Pro (fully maxed-out session)

Warm-up (from 1st working set = 75 kg): 1st warm-up set 37.5 kg × 6; 2nd warm-up set 75% of 75 kg = 56.25 kg. For a barbell exercise, 56.25 kg is an exact tie and is rounded down to 55 kg according to Section 8, i.e. 55 kg × 5.

Each set at its last ladder step:

| Set | Weight × reps | Origin (buffer) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 75 kg × 6 | 75%, 5% |
| 2 | 80 kg × 5 | 80%, 5% |
| 3 | 85 kg × 3 | 85%, 5% |
| 4 | 90 kg × 2 → switch exercise | 90%, 5% |

### 9.2 Advanced (squat, fully maxed-out session)

Warm-up (from 1st working set = 70 kg): 1st warm-up set 35 kg × 6 (50%); 2nd warm-up set 52.5 kg × 5 (75%).

Each set at its last ladder step (rating only after set 3; no adjustment within the session):

| Set | Weight × reps | Origin (buffer) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 70 kg × 6 (fixed 70%) | 70%, 10% |
| 2 | 75 kg × 5 (max. 75%) | 75%, 10% |
| 3 | 80 kg × 3 (max. 80%) | 80%, 10% |

Example progression for set 3 (each after "completed", sets 1 and 2 already at final value): 77.5 kg × 3 → 77.5 kg × 5 → 80 kg × 1 → … → 80 kg × 3 (final value → switch exercise).
